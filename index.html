Research

Keeping reward-hacking detectors reliable

Can we train a coding agent without losing the ability to detect when it cheats?

Built a research pipeline that trains coding agents on Python tasks with visible tests, then checks whether apparent success transfers to hidden tests. The experiments compare standard reinforcement learning with activation anchoring—a constraint that keeps selected internal representations close to the original model.

Research takeaway. At 7B, probe anchoring preserved detector calibration across three seeds. Evidence that it prevents hacking is still preliminary; a random-direction anchor also worked in the seed-0 control.

Research experiments · Limited seed counts; no claim of a proven general defense.

How stable are an AI model’s preferences?

Do small changes inside a language model change what it appears to value?

Built an experiment that adjusts internal activations along refusal, user-agreement, and random directions, then measures choices between outcomes. Preference models turn those choices into a comparable value ranking, while fluency checks help separate meaningful preference changes from degraded model behavior.

Research takeaway. An initial AI-autonomy preference shift depended on the estimation method and did not generalize robustly across models. Follow-up work uses validated estimators and preregistered replication tests.

Research in progress · Initial effects remain method-sensitive.

Whose preferences did the model learn?

Does preference training teach a model the regional preferences of its human raters?

Built a controlled preference-learning study using PRISM human-feedback data grouped by rater residence. Separately fine-tuned models on regional subsets, inspected internal representations and response style, and tested whether each model better matched held-out preferences from the same region.

Research takeaway. The held-out regional advantage was not reliably detected, including in Qwen and UK–continental Europe follow-ups. The study distinguishes detectable representation or style changes from actual preference matching.

Research experiments · Null results are part of the finding.

Small language models for vulnerability triage

Can a small language model turn a vulnerability description into useful security labels?

Explored vulnerability triage by comparing TF-IDF classifiers with LoRA-tuned Phi-3.5-mini and Qwen2.5 models on public CVE descriptions. The task predicts severity, CVSS score and vector components, and CWE weakness categories, using 2022–2023 data for training and validation and 2024 data for testing.

Research takeaway. The TF-IDF baseline reached about 67% severity accuracy and a 0.97 score MAE on the full test set. LoRA results were exploratory: only 12 examples were evaluated, and longer training showed overfitting and mode collapse.

Exploring sums of squares and polygonal numbers

Vital signs for contactless biometric authentication

Can radar-detected cardiac patterns help identify a person without physical contact?

Developed a biometric-authentication prototype using an off-the-shelf mmWave radar. Processed radio signals to isolate cardiac motion, extracted cardiac features using signal-processing methods, and used a machine-learning classifier to distinguish users.

Research takeaway. The 2023 conference abstract reports over 98% authentication accuracy in an experiment with 12 users. This is a small experimental study, rather than evidence of deployment-scale security.
